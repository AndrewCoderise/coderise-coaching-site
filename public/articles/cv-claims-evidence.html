Every line on your CV is a claim. Where is the evidence?

Andrew Basley
Tech Recruiter and former Head of HR · 19 November 2026

Read your CV as if you were marking a lab report. Every sentence is a claim about you. The question for each one is the same: what is the evidence, and could a stranger check it?

Most CVs fail this test in the first three lines.

Claims without evidence

"Passionate, results-driven engineer with strong communication skills." That sentence makes three claims and supports none of them. It is also on thousands of other CVs, so it tells a recruiter nothing about you.

Compare it with: "Built a retrieval system that answers support questions from 400 internal documents, used daily by a team of twelve." That is one claim, and it carries its own proof. A recruiter can picture the work, and an interviewer can ask about it.

The rule is simple. If a line could be copied onto someone else's CV and still be true, it is not evidence. Cut it or replace it.

What counts as evidence

  • What you built or did, named specifically. "A script that reconciles two billing exports", not "AI solutions".
  • The tools you used, by their real names. These are the words you are searched for.
  • The scale: how many users, records, requests, people or pounds.
  • The result: what changed because you did it, with a number if you have one.
  • Your part: what you did, as distinct from what the team did.

If you do not have a number, say what happened. "Still in use a year later" is evidence. "Adopted by two other teams" is evidence.

How it is written matters as much as what is written

The same fact can be found or missed depending on the words around it.

Use the term the employer uses. If adverts in your field say "machine learning engineer" and "RAG", write those, even if your team called it something else. A search matches words, not meaning.

Put the fact first. "Reduced processing time from four hours to twenty minutes by rewriting the pipeline" beats "Responsible for a range of pipeline improvements that led to efficiencies". The first survives a glance. The second does not.

Be honest about your level. A graduate claiming to have "architected enterprise systems" on a summer internship invites the question they least want. "Built and tested" is believable, and believable gets interviews.

The test

Take your CV and highlight every line that names something specific and checkable. Then look at what is left. That is the part doing no work.

Recruiters are not looking for the most impressive adjectives. We are looking for reasons to believe you. Give us evidence and we can put you forward. Give us claims and we move to the next one.

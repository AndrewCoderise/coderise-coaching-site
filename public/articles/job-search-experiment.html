Treat your job search like an experiment

Andrew Basley
Career coach and CV writer · 19 November 2026

If you have sent fifty applications and heard nothing, you do not have fifty failures. You have one result, repeated fifty times, and it is telling you something specific.

Most people respond to silence by sending more of the same. A scientist would not. They would ask what the result means and change one thing to find out.

Find where it breaks

A job search has stages, and each one tests a different thing.

StageWhat it testsIf you fail here, look at
Application to first responseWhether you are found and shortlistedYour CV and LinkedIn profile
First call to interviewWhether your story holds up in conversationHow you explain your experience and what you want
Interview to offerWhether they believe you can do the jobYour examples, preparation and questions
Offer to acceptanceWhether the deal is rightHow you negotiate

Count what happened at each stage. If nothing comes back from applications, practising interview answers is wasted effort. If you get interviews and no offers, rewriting your CV again will not help. Fix the stage that is failing.

Change one thing at a time

Say you rewrite your CV, switch to different roles and start messaging recruiters, all in the same week. Replies improve. Which change did it? You cannot know, so you cannot do more of it.

Change one thing, send a fair batch and compare. Two applications prove nothing. Twenty with the old CV against twenty with the new one tells you something real.

Write down what you expect

Before each change, write one sentence: "I think I am not being found because my profile does not use the words in the adverts." Then test that. If replies do not move, the guess was wrong. That is useful, because you can cross it off and test the next one.

Keep a record

A simple sheet is enough: role, date, which version of your CV, where you applied and what came back. After a month you will see patterns you could not have guessed. Certain job titles answer and others never do. Applications through one route land and another route goes nowhere.

Why this matters more now

Hiring has become a volume game on both sides, and volume without feedback is noise. The candidates who get hired fastest are rarely the ones who apply the most. They are the ones who work out quickest what is not working and stop doing it.

Silence feels personal. It is data. Treat it that way and the search stops being something that happens to you.

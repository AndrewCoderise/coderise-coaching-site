Hiring changed. Most CV advice did not.

Andrew Basley
Tech Recruiter and former Head of HR · 19 November 2026

Most of the CV advice you will find was written for a world where a person read your application. That world has gone, and advice built for it now works against you.

What changed

Applying used to cost effort. You found the advert, wrote a letter and posted or emailed it. That effort kept the numbers down, so a recruiter could read what arrived.

Applying now costs two clicks. A single advert pulls in hundreds of applications, often thousands, and most of them are from people who are nowhere near the brief. The good candidates are still in there. They are just buried.

Nobody can read a thousand CVs, so companies have changed how they cope. Screening software sorts and filters before a person looks. Recruiters search a database for the words they need instead of reading the pile. When a human does open your CV, it gets a glance, not a read.

What that means for you

You now have two readers, and they want different things.

The first is a search. It does not admire your layout or infer what you meant. It matches words. If the role asks for Python and Kubernetes and your CV says "modern cloud tooling", you are not in the results.

The second is a tired person with a long list. They decide in seconds whether you are worth the next minute. They look at the top third of the first page, your most recent job title and whether the words from their brief are staring back at them.

A CV written to impress a careful reader fails both. It hides the keywords inside elegant sentences, and it saves the best material for page two.

The advice that has aged badly

  • "Stand out with a creative design." Columns, graphics and text boxes are exactly what parsing software scrambles.
  • "Keep it to one page whatever happens." Cutting the specific tools and results to hit a page count removes the things you are searched for.
  • "Tell your story." Nobody reaches the story if the first glance does not earn it.

What works now

Write for the search first and the glance second. Use the exact terms the role uses. Put the strongest evidence where the eye lands. Make every line carry a fact.

None of this is about gaming a system. It is about being findable by people who are looking for someone like you and cannot see you. I have been on the other side of that search for 15 years, and the candidates I miss are almost never the weak ones. They are the invisible ones.
